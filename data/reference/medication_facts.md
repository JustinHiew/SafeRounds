## Using the pen

- **F01** Semaglutide for weight management comes as a liquid in a prefilled pen and is injected under the skin once a week, on the same day each week, at any time of day. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F02** It is injected into the upper arm, thigh or stomach, and the injection site should be rotated. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F03** Treatment usually starts at a low dose that the doctor increases after 4 weeks, and possibly again after another 4 weeks. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F04** Before injecting, the liquid should be clear, colourless and free of particles; do not use it if it is discoloured, cloudy, thickened or past its expiry date. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F05** Needles and pens must never be reused or shared, and used needles go in a puncture-resistant container. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`

## Missed doses, overdose and stopping

- **F06** A missed dose should be injected within 2 days (48 hours) of when it was due; if the next scheduled dose is less than 2 days away, skip the missed dose and continue the usual schedule. Never take a double dose or two injections together to make up for a missed one. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F07** If more than the prescribed amount has been used, the person should get urgent medical advice; in Australia the Poisons Information Centre (13 11 26) gives advice 24 hours a day. [S1, S2, S7] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F08** Do not stop the medicine suddenly or without talking to the doctor first, even when feeling well. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F09** Weight-loss medicines do not cure obesity; after stopping, it is important to keep up new habits around eating, exercise and lifestyle. [S5] `verified 2026-10-08`

## Storage

- **F10** Pens are stored in the fridge at 2°C to 8°C and must not be frozen. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F11** The pen is kept in its container with the cap on, away from light and heat, and out of reach of children; it may be kept at room temperature for a limited time only, and the pharmacist or doctor can say how long. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`

## Common side effects

- **F12** Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, stomach pain, heartburn or reflux, burping, headache, runny nose or sore throat, tiredness, itchy or red skin where you inject, and hair loss. [S1, S2, S5] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F13** A pharmacist or doctor should be contacted if side effects are severe, bothersome or do not go away; otherwise keep taking the medicine unless advised to stop. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F14** It is best not to drink alcohol, because it can make nausea and vomiting worse. [S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F15** Recent diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, or being unable to drink liquids can cause dehydration and should be reported to the doctor. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`

## Serious side effects that need urgent medical care

- **F16** Ongoing pain that starts in the upper left or middle of the stomach and may spread to the back, with or without vomiting, can be a sign of pancreatitis and needs urgent care. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F17** Upper stomach pain, yellowing of the skin or eyes, fever, or clay-coloured stools can be signs of gallbladder or liver problems and need urgent care. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F18** Rash, itching, fainting or dizziness, swelling of the face, mouth, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing or swallowing can be signs of a serious allergic reaction and need urgent care. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F19** Passing less urine, or swelling of the legs, ankles or feet, needs urgent care. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F20** Changes in vision, including sudden loss of vision, need urgent care; do not drive or use machinery while vision is affected. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F21** A lump or swelling in the neck, hoarseness, difficulty swallowing or shortness of breath can be signs of a thyroid problem and need urgent care. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F22** A rapid or pounding heartbeat needs urgent care. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`

## Mood

- **F23** Some weight-loss medicines can affect mood and may increase the risk of depression. New or worsening depression or thoughts of suicide should be raised with a doctor; anyone in immediate danger should call triple zero (000), and Lifeline (13 11 14) offers support. [S5] `verified 2026-10-08` (the source says "some weight loss medicines", not semaglutide specifically)

## Who should not use it, and other medicines

- **F24** Semaglutide caused thyroid tumours in rats; it is not known whether it does so in humans. A doctor will likely advise against it for anyone with a personal or family history of thyroid tumours, thyroid cancer or MEN 2. [S1] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F25** It may not be suitable for someone who has had pancreatitis. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F26** It is not used during pregnancy; anyone who becomes pregnant while using it should contact their doctor straight away. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F27** It should be stopped at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy, and contraception is needed if there is a chance of pregnancy. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F28** It is not used while breastfeeding. [S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F29** Low blood sugar can cause dizziness, sweating, hunger, shakiness, headache and a fast heartbeat; it is more likely when semaglutide is used with other diabetes medicines such as insulin or gliclazide, whose dose the doctor may need to adjust. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F30** Some medicines may not mix well with semaglutide, including warfarin, levothyroxine, insulin, metformin and gliclazide; the doctor should know about all other medicines and supplements. [S1, S2] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F31** Because it slows how quickly the stomach empties, anyone giving a general anaesthetic, sedative or procedure, including dental surgery, should be told the person uses semaglutide. [S1, S2, S5] `verified 2026-10-08`

## Nutrition, movement and cost

- **F32** Adults aged 19 to 64 should do at least 150 minutes (2½ hours) of moderate-intensity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity a week, plus strengthening activities on at least 2 days a week. [S3] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F33** Weight loss can mean losing muscle as well as fat; people using injected weight-loss medicines should make sure they eat enough protein and exercise, including resistance training. Whether these medicines cause more muscle loss than weight loss alone is unclear. [S6] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F34** Adults should eat at least 5 portions of a variety of fruit and vegetables a day and drink 6 to 8 glasses of fluid a day. [S4] `verified 2026-10-08`
- **F35** In Australia, medicines prescribed for weight loss are not currently covered by Medicare. [S5] `verified 2026-10-08`
